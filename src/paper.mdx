## Overview

Vision-and-language navigation requires an agent to connect route instructions with what it sees. Standard navigation metrics measure how well it moves, but do not reveal which visual evidence supports its actions.

**LandMarkVLN** connects each instructed landmark to current and historical observations, masks of the same physical object, and local reference actions. We use it to audit eight released navigation VLA models from two complementary perspectives: **where visual attribution falls**, and **whether editing the landmark changes the output more than a matched control**.

The central finding: acceptable local actions and broad visual responsiveness are not, by themselves, reliable evidence that a model relies on the instructed landmark.

</HighlightedSection>

<h2 id="dataset">One landmark, linked through time</h2>

Each sample joins a navigation instruction and a singular landmark phrase with a current RGB observation, recent observation history, temporally aligned masks of the **same physical landmark**, and a local expert-action horizon. The dataset is constructed from the continuous-environment releases of R2R and RxR, using English instructions.

<div class="dataset-fields not-prose" aria-label="Contents of a LandMarkVLN sample">
  <span>Instruction + landmark</span><span>Current + historical views</span><span>Same-instance masks</span><span>Local reference actions</span>
</div>

### Three stages of construction

| Stage | What is checked | Output |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Text-only selection | The phrase denotes one resolvable physical object instance. | 118,706 candidate identities |
| Current-view grounding | The target is visible, unique, and geometrically usable. | 19,742 current-grounded identities |
| Temporal verification | Visibility and same-instance identity are checked across history. | **7,228 intervention-ready samples** |

The current-view stage evaluates 350,660 candidate–frame pairs generated from the text-selected identities. Counts across stages use different units.

<h2 id="findings">What the audit finds</h2>

The evaluation covers **OneVLA, InternVLA-N1, OmniVLA, Uni-NaVid, FantasyVLN, StreamVLN, VAMOS, and TIC-VLA**, spanning current-only and history-aware inputs, and discrete and continuous actions.

<div class="finding-list not-prose">
  <article><div class="finding-count">2 <span>/ 60</span></div><div><h3>Localization rarely tracks acceptable actions.</h3><p>Two of 60 vision-action evaluations support a positive association between landmark localization and acceptable local actions after model-wise Holm correction.</p></div></article>
  <article><div class="finding-count">23 <span>/ 23</span></div><div><h3>Models respond to broad visual changes.</h3><p>Every evaluated intervention configuration shows a positive mean response to the full-frame edit.</p></div></article>
  <article><div class="finding-count">6 <span>/ 23</span></div><div><h3>Landmark-specific sensitivity has limited support.</h3><p>Six configurations support a larger response to landmark edits than to matched non-landmark control edits after Holm correction within each native output family.</p></div></article>
</div>

These denominators count evaluations and configurations, not individual models. The 60 vision-action evaluations combine 20 model–temporal-scope pairs with three attribution estimators. The intervention analysis uses 23 configurations, including separate output branches for InternVLA-N1.
